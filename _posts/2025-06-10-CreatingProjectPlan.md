Hey team!

So you’ve bravely entered the world of **HackHPC\@ADMI25**, armed with snacks, dreams, and just enough Python knowledge to be dangerous. You’re not alone—I'm your friendly mentor, here to help you **plan your project like pros** and *not* panic before your big 10-minute final presentation. 🧠💻🎤

## 🗓️ Day-by-Day Game Plan

### **🔬 Day 1: Meet, Greet, and Pick Your Paper**

> Goal: Get organized, pick a reproducibility target, and build hype.

* **Form your team** and settle on a paper from **ICSE 2023** or **SC24**. If it has code, data, and mystery—perfect.
* Set up your **GitHub repo**. Add a README with:

  * A short project description (think: "We tried to reproduce this cool result and here's what we found").
  * Your goals (e.g., scoring the paper, testing code, and building a killer portal).
* Assign roles: coder, scribe, portal builder, slide guru, and *vibe curator* (someone's gotta DJ).
* End the day with your 1-minute intro slide (team name, song, Zoom background, and member pics). Make it epic.

---

### **⚙️ Day 2: Planning & First Contact (With Code)**

> Goal: Create your project plan and dive into the reproducibility artifact like Indiana Jones with a laptop.

* Draft a **2-slide project plan**:

  * List team roles and at least 5 tasks.
  * Schedule daily check-ins (yes, even if it's just to say "we broke everything, help").
* Start evaluating:

  * Is the paper accessible?
  * Is the code shared and runnable?
  * Can you find and use the data?
* Bonus: Try running the code. If it works first try, buy a lottery ticket.

---

### **🧪 Day 3: Midpoint Check-In & Metric Mayhem**

> Goal: Score your paper, build your scorecard, and log early wins (or weird errors).

* Update your project plan slides with progress and problems.
* Develop your **scorecard**:

  * Paper availability: open-access or locked behind 12 paywalls?
  * Code/software availability: usable repo or cryptic zip file with no README?
  * Data availability: link to a dataset or…404?
  * Computer requirements: does it need a supercomputer or a toaster?
  * Add 3 more custom metrics—get creative! (e.g., “Documentation That Didn’t Make Us Cry”)
* Start drafting your **poster** and sketch your **portal layout**.
* Reminder: Commit early, commit often. GitHub is your friend.

---

### **📊 Day 4: Portal Magic & Poster Polish**

> Goal: Make it pretty, make it clear, and make it reproducible.

* Build your **website or portal**:

  * Display scorecard results (bar charts, tables, memes—get visual).
  * Explain each metric in simple, smart terms.
  * Include your team’s bios, photos, and links (LinkedIn/GitHub flex time).
* Finalize your **poster (PDF)** and submit it to **Gateways 2025**. Screenshot that confirmation—**that’s +5 points**!
* Document everything in your repo. README, comments, structure—it’s what reproducibility is all about.

---

### **🎤 Day 5: Final Countdown & Presentation Power-Up**

> Goal: Deliver the goods and tell your story.

* Final check-in: what’s done, what’s shiny, and what’s duct-taped but functional?
* Your **final presentation (PDF)** should cover:

  * Team names and faces (smiling is optional, but encouraged).
  * Tools used (Docker? Slurm? Jupyter? VS Code and vibes?)
  * Your scorecard results.
  * The "how" and "why" behind your scores.
  * What went wrong, what you learned, and what you’d do next time.
* Practice your timing—**10 minutes max**! Split it up among team members.

---

## 🧠 Pro Tips From Your Hackathon Mentor

* **Don’t stress perfection**—this is a prototype, not a PhD defense.
* **Commit your work early**—GitHub doesn’t wait for anyone.
* **Be kind to each other**—hackathons are intense, but teamwork makes reproducibility work.
* **Celebrate the small wins**—even if it’s just figuring out the author's Python version.
